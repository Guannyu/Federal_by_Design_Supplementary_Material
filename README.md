# Federated by Design --- Supplementary Material

Supplementary material for **"Federated by Design: A Framework for the Central Authority in Federated Data Governance"** (G. Chen, University of Twente).

The article specifies what the central governance function *is* in a federated data governance arrangement. Specifically describing its authority basis, its functions, its accountability, and how an organization moves from centralized to federated governance. It was built through design science research: a systematic literature review of 40 studies, three generative expert interviews, and six expert evaluations.

This repository holds the material behind that study: the full framework specification, the review protocol, and the interview analysis artifacts.

## Contents

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Framework_with_Extension.pdf` | The complete framework in one document. Every element is marked **CORE** (mandatory) or **EXTENSION** (contingent, adopted under stated conditions). The article presents the core; this is the core plus the eleven extension components and one recorded candidate. |
| `Literature_Review.pdf` | The systematic literature review behind the article's problem investigation. Contains the full review protocol, including objectives, research questions, search strings, databases, inclusion/exclusion criteria, ten-item quality checklist, extraction and synthesis strategy. Alongside the thematic results, the interview protocol and question set, and the 40 included studies with their per-item quality scores (Appendix A). |
| `interview_material/Design_Phase_Traceability.pdf` | Component-level traceability for the design phase. Maps each of the 92 design-relevant components voiced across the three design interviews (D1–D3) to its disposition in the framework: carried directly, carried in generalized form, partially carried, not carried, or context. |
| `interview_material/Coding_Codebook.pdf` | The coding scheme for the validation phase: the confirm / refine / extend / challenge valence system and its qualifiers, the 35 deductive codes derived from the framework, the 17 emergent codes added during coding, and per-code application counts across the six evaluation interviews (E1–E6). |
| `interview_material/Validation_Matrix.pdf` | Cross-case validation matrix. Every framework element against every evaluation expert, with the valence assigned, the segment references, and a cross-case reading of the evidence. This is where the article's coverage claims can be checked element by element. |

## A note on the interview data

Interview transcripts are **not** published. Participation was voluntary, ethical approval was obtained before data collection, recordings were used only for transcription and then deleted, and transcripts were anonymized. Participants appear here only under the codes used in the article: **D1–D3** for the design phase and **E1–E6** for the validation phase.
